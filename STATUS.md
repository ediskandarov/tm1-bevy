# Playable arena status

The macOS application launches XARENA1 from the verified JP BIN/CUE. The player selects one of twelve vehicles and fights a computer-controlled opponent on the selected difficulty. The arena includes acceleration, reversing, steering, turbo, tire contact, bridges, collision damage, police pursuit and attacks, gun heat, missiles, pickups and regeneration. Health, selected ammunition and gun heat are visible in the chase view. Zero health costs one life. R restores the stage-entry checkpoint. CDDA and decoded VAB voices play through Bevy.

The player completed a manual keyboard playthrough, heard game sounds and reached the **Arena cleared** victory screen. Keyboard play is the accepted input configuration for this milestone.

Sweet Tooth, Yellow Jacket, Darkside, Hammerhead, Outlaw, Crimson Fury, Warthog, Mr. Grimm, Pit Viper, Thumper, Spectre and Road Kill
load their own driving profile, collision geometry, three damage models,
special weapon and audio programs. Each selection was launched on the
Apple M3 Pro Metal backend and its chase-view frame inspected. Arena draws its opponent from the original card order with the BIOS random sequence starting at seed 1. Restart restores the same draw and simulation seed. All six arenas support their opening encounters. Rooftops continues into the Minion encounter. Winning advances the single-player campaign. Defeating Minion opens the JP reward screen and scrolling credits. The title screen leads into a keyboard contest selector. The full retail menu and multiplayer are outstanding.

## Vehicle selection

The one-player garage reads the twelve showroom models and occupant portraits from CARS.DMD and CARS.TMS. The selection loop follows the original card order, 11-unit heading increment and 22-field slide, with showroom headlights disabled. Each card plays its VAB signature and engine rev. CAR INFO opens that card’s SCNDB biography from the JP disc. A disc-backed test traverses all twelve biographies, wraps around the roster and exits to the title. The software projection shares the ending renderer. Exact original lighting and ordering-table composition remain unverified. A Metal capture of Outlaw is reproducible with `--garage --car outlaw --demo-screenshot local/garage-outlaw.png 40`.

## Single-player battle setup

Starting a contest or advancing a stage plays the original stage announcement and deals the selected opponents' indexed vehicle cards over the preceding scene. The deal consumes one BIOS random value per opponent before the battle initializes the cars. The animation advances at 60 fields per second and plays the card throw from UACORE.VAB. The host loads stage data synchronously, so disc seek times are not reproduced. To inspect a five-card deal, use `--garage --level freeway --demo-transition-screenshot local/freeway-opponents.png 45`.

Selecting a vehicle opens SCRNE21P.TIM with its 128 × 64 indexed card, followed by START, OPTIONS and EXIT. OPTIONS uses the disc difficulty labels and four control diagrams. Changing the layout before the encounter configures the core and keyboard mapping. The password editor and retry screen use the retail text positions, colors and blinking underscore. P and D in the garage remain keyboard shortcuts. Disc-backed tests follow the normal menu path into Arena 5 with a changed difficulty and control layout, and check both setup idle deadlines. To capture the battle card, run with `--garage --car outlaw --demo-battle-screenshot local/battle-outlaw.png 50`.

## Opening sequence and cinema

A normal launch displays the three original JP startup TIMs and plays the Sony logo and title movies in order. The shell reads each STR as a bounded range of raw sectors on track 1. FFmpeg decodes MDEC frames at 320 × 240 and interleaved XA sound for Bevy. Video frames stream through a pipe, and the decoder is reaped on skip, completion or shutdown. The original `MOVIE.c` lookahead leaves the last numbered frame undisplayed. Sound and image pause while the window is unfocused.

After 1,201 idle fields the title cycles through the battle movie, the contest history, and the vehicle demonstration on successive visits. History scrolls the 31 lines of HISTORY.TXT over SCRNCALY.TIM. Natural completion introduces the twelve competitors, while Enter skips to the title movie. The vehicle demonstration uses SCRND2.TIM, rotates through the original card order at 361 fields per vehicle, and plays each signature over the title theme. A disc-backed integration test covers startup order, logo skip timing, title frame changes, the theme handoff and the idle demo. To inspect the title movie in a deterministic Metal run, use `--menu --demo-screenshot local/title-cinema.png 378`.

## Defeat and Minion introduction

Losing the last life interrupts the level music, plays the engine rev and Sweet Tooth signature, shows the original “YOU LOSE!” lettering over the battle and returns to the title. A new contest starts in Arena with three lives. The return transition plays `UAMOVIES/TMTTL15S.STR` before the title theme starts. A disc-backed test traverses the defeat, title and fresh contest in one Bevy world.

The Rooftops boss introduction uses the five original lines, font and screen coordinates from `screenBossCar`. Held input must be released before its five-second timer starts. The intermediate lost-life overlay follows the 30-field red ramp and 60-field hold in `screenLostALife`. The Bevy overlay uses alpha blending over the live render target. To capture the boss message on Metal, use `--level rooftops --demo-boss-screenshot local/boss-rooftops.png 36`.

## Contest ending

After the Rooftops victory delay, Calypso awards one of four passwords. The screen uses the disc TIM, font atlas and JP executable glyph metrics. The credits load CREDITS.TXT, the selected CARSEND model and its disc backdrop. Outlaw and Road Kill use their own camera motion, signature timing and CDDA tracks. Enter advances the reward and skips the credits. Up and Down change the scroll speed. After credits, Easy and Medium return to the title screen. Hard continues to Arena 5 with the same driver, remaining lives, inventory and opponent deck.

The reward and Outlaw credits were captured on Apple M3 Pro Metal using a temporary forced Rooftops victory. The captures verify the two screens independently of a complete campaign playthrough. The ending renderer has not been compared pixel-for-pixel against console output. The title movie remains open.

## Difficulty

D on the vehicle selection screen cycles the difficulty. AI strength scales to 60, 100 or 120 on Easy, Medium or Hard. The weapon profiles change special and directional attack chances. Incoming player damage uses the retail integer scaling, and both weapon pickup and health stand regeneration use the selected delays. Difficulty persists across respawns, stage transitions and the return to the title.

The retail comparison covers AI spawn records across all three difficulties and battle traces on Easy and Hard in Arena, Warehouse and the Rooftops boss encounter. A frontend test starts the Hard credits with one life and checks the transition to the five-opponent bonus contest.

## Warehouse

Launch with `--level warehouse` to drive WH1 or WH2, selected by the original vehicle-dependent shell rules, against all three original opponents for the selected player. The game reads the level geometry, sky and textures from the disc, spawns the player at the original Warehouse start, and plays CDDA track 4. Warehouse uses its own trigger points, bridges, curbs, potholes and AI lane selection. The encounter can be restarted from the keyboard. The HUD shows the nearest surviving opponent and the number of rivals left. The round ends after all three rivals are defeated or the player is wrecked. Winning advances to Freeway.

## Freeway

Launch with `--level freeway`. The selected vehicle determines the XFWY1 or XFWY2 database, textures and five opponents. The level uses 50 routing points, 18 bridge records, two potholes and CDDA track 2. Metal captures on the M3 Pro show Yellow Jacket and Outlaw on the highway. The keyboard restart fixture replays the same five-car lineup.

## City Park

Launch with `--level city-park` for the six-opponent encounter. Both park databases load with their corresponding textures, original player and opponent starts, 42 routing points, 54 bridge records, 16 curbs, the active pothole and the rectangular slick. Single-player City Park uses CDDA track 4. The M3 Pro Metal captures show Yellow Jacket on PARK2 and Hammerhead on PARK1.

## Cyburbia

Launch with `--level cyburbia` to fight eight opponents in SUBURB1 or SUBURB2. The port uses the original routing and ground-height tables, daytime headlight settings and CDDA track 6. The street geometry belongs to a visibility controller. Each frame selects its inside or outside branch using the camera position and the original oriented box test. Metal captures of Outlaw and Yellow Jacket show the residential streets on both variants.

## Rooftops

Launch with `--level rooftops` for the opening three-opponent battle. The level loads ROOF1 or ROOF2, uses 49 routing points and 14 ground groups, disables AI lane following, and plays CDDA track 8. After the third rival falls, the simulation continues through the original 180-field delay and spawns Minion on the roof selected by the player’s ground group. A player below height 6,400 respawns on the starting roof. The fourth opponent uses front and rear machine guns and a special that combines two swarm missiles, a projectile, a taser and a front flamethrower. R restarts the three-rival opening. A Metal capture with all three rivals set to zero health at startup showed Minion at 250 health and one survivor out of four. The five-second Minion introduction freezes simulation and displays the retail message. Enter advances to the fight. Helicopter View follows the moving car above the roofs.

## Single-player campaign

A contest starts with three lives. The first five victories advance through Warehouse, Freeway, City Park, Cyburbia and Rooftops in the same window. Each stage loads its own collision geometry, textures, opponent lineup, audio voices and CD track. A victory waits for the original 180-field return delay to expire. The new stage restores health, resets gun heat, grants 20 special charges and retains ordinary ammunition and the selected weapon. A manual restart replays that stage entry. The port uses reproducible RNG across stages. The retail shell also samples a hardware timer.

On player death, simulation continues through the return delay. While lives remain, the player respawns in the same encounter with the default weapon loadout. The terminal defeat freezes after the third loss. Visual checks on Metal used a fixture that started the opening opponents at zero health to trigger the Arena-to-Warehouse transition and the Rooftops introduction.

## Native presentation

On the Apple M3 Pro (18 GPU cores, macOS 15.8.3) the native Metal capture showed the taxi, arena textures, projectiles and HUD in a 1280 × 800 logical window. With the normal 50 ms simulation clock and scripted input, the app presented 300 steps over 15.0046 seconds with 4.5956 ms remaining. The final frame showed the police colliding with the taxi and the taxi down to 66 health. Median / p95 timings were 0.0269 / 0.0447 ms per simulation step, 0.7122 / 0.7644 ms for CPU geometry preparation, and 0.8596 / 1.1361 ms for the GPU graph interval. There were 80 CPU samples after warmup, 79 valid GPU timestamp pairs and 310,704 KiB RSS at capture. Engine and music playback cursors advanced. [Native run output](evidence/metal-realtime.txt). The separate demo capture advances one simulation step per presentation frame to compare two render paths at the same point.

The arena, both cars and effects share a perspective camera and depth buffer during normal play. The HUD is an overlay. Each opaque vehicle body is submitted once as a retained mesh. The original arena enables headlight groups 24 and 54 on the taxi and police car. Their beam texture pages request additive blending. The renderer modulates light in the texture's display color space and adds it to the scene for STP texels. A black texel in the beam adds zero light. Non-STP texels replace the destination color. To reproduce the collision capture, run with `--demo-screenshot local/collision.png 300`.

Texture zero is transparent. Semi-transparent primitives honor the texture word's STP bit with an alpha approximation. The arena imports eight billboard instances with original center, extents and UVs; the primitive camera expands them in view space. The crowd display loads both textured DMD branches and changes pose every 16 simulation steps. Its selected child matched 512 successive calls to the retail HIER routine. The port clocks this cosmetic animation once per simulation step, including while off camera. The original clocks it when hierarchy traversal visits the node. Other PS1 blend modes, including subtraction, and exact ordering-table placement are incomplete. Flamethrower particles use the 16-frame Aburst animation read from XARENA1 and its additive texture pages. The port tracks flame bursts in a dedicated pool. The original shares that pool with other explosion types. Missiles use the arena DMD models (201 and 312) and the eight-frame contrail sprite set (652). Other combat effects still use replacement geometry. The default chase camera and HUD layout remain port-specific.

The arena plays CDDA track 3 and VAB programs for the taxi engine, gun, impact, explosion and missile launch. Pitched engine looping uses the ADPCM loop marker; shots retain bank stereo with five listener-relative pan positions. The port caps effects at 22 concurrent voices plus music and engine. SPU reverb, ADSR, noise and exact voice priorities differ. Original SOUND.c uses key status for voice management. The tested car and weapon updates agree while audio output endpoints are stubbed. Metal smoke reported live audio sinks. The player confirmed audible game sounds during play. Relative music and effect levels were not individually evaluated.

## Source baseline and scope

A fresh offline linux/amd64 build of pinned source `fb8f9dd4c3aff275893c2573c432a94f644e9970` with pinned maspsx produced ROM SHA-256 `32f36dbb4e9451d767869cd7c6f226d35fa97884fd39c0409ecdf4469f974b43`. The retail reference ROM hash is `95b57efc67c0625f98c76442d6c939b509e5e8b375aed9c344c858aaeccb5bc9`. Both ROMs are 610,304 bytes long, with 325,480 differing byte positions. The first difference is at file offset 12616 (zero based). Differential tests use the verified retail executable directly. The project's fixed source snapshot does not establish a byte-matching rebuild.

The native run reported zero gamepads. Physical controller testing is deferred. Full-game parity, other arenas, exact mixed rendering occlusion and exact SPU behavior are outside the evidence above.
