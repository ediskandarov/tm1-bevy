# Comparisons with the JP executable

## Reference execution

The test interpreter executes instructions loaded from the verified retail executable. The two vehicle records are copied to PS1-layout memory and compared byte by byte. The RNG starts at 1 for spawning and is then shared by both cars. Tests provide the BIOS random service, relevant GTE instructions, and explicit calls or stubs for presentation endpoints. These are controlled function-level scenarios. They do not execute an entire booted PlayStation or compare PCM and draw packets.

| Scenario | Observed result |
| --- | --- |
| Player dynamics | A 600-step fixed-three-field drive, 600-step variable-field drives and a 360-step ramp run. All 0x4a4 player bytes and the RNG are compared after each of the 29,760 updates. Ramp peak 164 with all four tires airborne. |
| Twelve player vehicles | For each vehicle the independently initialized 288-byte stat block matches the JP executable. The same 600-step drive runs on all four controller layouts with alternating 3/4/5/3 fields. The reference reads the physical button assignments from retail uaPadInit and GetCtlPad. Every car reaches forward and reverse speed and enters a braking turn. Position, heading and displayed speed agree across layouts at every step. A separate firing scenario compares both car records, RNG and three steps of the special projectile trajectory for the six projectile-based specials. |
| Taxi and police together | 1,200 consecutive updates of Yellow Jacket and the police car on Arena. The driver accelerates, holds the gun after step ten and steers left on steps 24–39. After step 360 the input repeats a 160-update sequence with acceleration, coasting, reverse, right steering and hard turns. The field count cycles through 4/5/3/3. Both complete car records and the random state agree after every update. |
| Player state between arenas | A hard-campaign fixture starts each of the twelve vehicles on Arena, then takes it through all five stage changes and into the bonus arena. At each destination it compares the complete player record and RNG against retail CarInit after advancing with damage, a selected weapon, spent special ammunition and gun heat. It also compares the new-life initialization after a simulated death. A Bevy integration test carries the far camera across the five regular transitions and checks the positional sound listener before the first driving update. |
| Extended campaign encounters | The same 1,200-update Yellow Jacket drive matches the JP executable on all six campaign arenas, the bonus arena and the separate Minion encounter. Each update compares the player and opponent records, RNG, wheel animation, gun and contact sound requests, battle music state and victory flag. These replays start each encounter independently. |
| Opponent selection and initialization | Compared all twelve Warehouse lineups and 36 successive Arena draws with retail uaSelectOpponents, including deck resets and RNG. For each regular opponent, compared all 0x560 bytes after AICarInit and AICarSetDefaultWeapons, and the resulting RNG. For each of the twelve Warehouse player choices, initialized the full three-opponent group and compared each slot, reserved starting point, complete car record and final RNG with retail. |
| Group collisions and restart | Compared 250 Warehouse point hits with retail, covering separated and overlapping cars and pairs of excluded IDs. Replayed 180 steps through the keyboard frontend after a round restart and compared all three opponents, player health and RNG. |
| Battle selection | Checked the PlayGame scheduler against the JP instructions in 128 combinations of opponent count, occupied attack slots, surviving vehicles and elapsed fields. Compared the wait counter and every opponent record after selection. |
| Opponent radar | For every regular vehicle, checked contact color, screen position and all four edge directions against retail hudAddRadarSig at seven headings and fourteen offsets (1,176 cases). Warehouse Metal capture shows the three opponents in their original colors. V toggles the overlay. Active Warehouse recharge stations flash on the radar. |
| Vehicle sound flags | After each player or enabled AI update, the simulation captures the car’s position, skid, impact and pending explosion and signature flags before its damage model runs. The same snapshot supplies speed, gear and turbo for the engine request. Campaign and duel playback retain the requested pitch even if a later actor changes the car before the frame ends. Each explosion or signature request clears its flag. The car explosion preserves horizontal pan at full range. Minion plays signature program 54 and special weapon program 56. Campaign replays compare each actor’s non-engine requests in order and source position with JP `UASetSoundFlags` at every step, alongside the complete car records. The same replays check the pitch and source position of every active actor’s engine request. Separate tests trigger both flags together on player and AI, check the following step is quiet for both requests, and verify the selected VAB programs for all thirteen cars. |
| Collision and skid audio | The tire skid flag and signed collision intensity in each car record drive the retail VAB skid and three crash programs. Repeated requests adjust the active crash or skid volume. The JP comparison checks 676 combinations across the full roster, player and AI layouts, both skid states and the crash thresholds. |
| Player engine | Each engine tone starts at its attack, repeats at its own ADPCM marker and follows the pitch range in the VAB. In single-player battle the camera controls both channels. The garage, reward, credits and defeat screens request a centered rev of 30. A Road Kill test compares both channels for both tones through a full attack and into the first loop in battle and on the reward screen. On Metal at Warehouse frame 110 the two player tones ran at 1.691× and 1.974× speed. Both drivers in split-screen also use the ADPCM loop markers. |
| Rival engines | The battle mixer loads the engine for each opponent, including Minion, from its disc VAB program. The AI sound flag enables playback. Each motor follows the car in stereo and changes pitch with its velocity, gear and turbo flags. The JP comparison checks 624 engine requests across 13 cars. A 15-second Warehouse run on Metal recorded all three rival engines playing at different speeds. Each VAB tone plays its own attack and loops from its ADPCM marker, with its own pitch range. The primary camera supplies both channel gains. On Warehouse at frame 110, three rival cars had four active tones at playback speeds from 1.323 to 4.115. |
| Pickup audio | Successful weapon pickups emit sound at the collecting player or rival. The 11 regular weapon pickup IDs and the turbo pickup use VAB program 36. Requests for the same pickup type share an active voice. The JP comparison checks source coordinates, program selection and silent cases for 14 tags with both collectors at two inventory levels. Pickup voices use the integer left and right levels from the listener transform. |
| Campaign voice allocation | Engine and effect tones share 24 voices. New or refreshed requests go through the range and priority partitions in JP `soundSortInstances`. Finishing tones keep unused slots in their original list order. The reference test compares every resulting pointer for 32 lists of up to 48 tones, including ties across the 24-voice boundary. In a Warehouse test ten four-tone explosions displace both Road Kill engine tones. A render update between simulation steps preserves the result. On the next simulation step both engine tones return and the oldest finishing tones retain the available slots. The Warehouse Metal capture at frame 110 recorded 23 audio sinks including music. |
| Campaign positional sound | Rival engines and battle effects use the primary gameplay camera for sound. Each simulation step retains its own effect channel levels. The port applies the JP view transform, signed coordinate limits, squared range and integer left/right volume calculation. Loud explosions zero the range while retaining horizontal direction. A reference test runs the retail view and mixer code at 16 orientations in chase, far, cockpit and detached views with seven source offsets, four volume settings and both range policies (3,584 channel pairs). Each campaign effect applies the two channel gains to the decoded VAB stereo samples at the start of each stereo frame. Repeated requests update the playing voice. A Warehouse integration test compares 2,048 output frames against the dry impact sample across a gain change. A separate Warehouse test moves the listener from one side of a running engine to the other and compares the stereo samples across both loop boundaries. A Cyburbia Metal run with Outlaw reported two playing rival engines at frame 110. |
| Campaign machine guns | The player and each active rival issue independent program 12 play and stop requests. Each vehicle has its own sound ID. Both tones play their attacks once and loop while the gun stays active. Both gun tones follow the firing car’s current channel levels without restarting their attacks. The 360-update encounter replays compare each actor’s requests against the JP executable across all campaign stages. A keyboard test covers release during cooldown and sustained fire through overheat. On Metal, both Road Kill gun sinks advanced from 215 ms to 540 ms between simulation frames 20 and 60 on Warehouse. |
| Turbo audio | Human driving physics issues play and stop requests for the shared JP sound ID 0x82. UACORE program 26 has four tones. Three repeat from their individual ADPCM loop markers and one finishes naturally. Releasing turbo or running out of fuel stops the sound. In split screen both drivers address the same ID in slot order. A keyboard test covers holding, releasing, running dry and simultaneous inputs. Campaign turbo uses the same per-tone integer volume calculation as duel turbo. The test varies the effects setting while retaining the four campaign decoders and checks both channels against the expected gain at each sample, including mute. A Warehouse run with temporary scripted turbo input on Metal verified four active audio sinks at 125 ms on simulation frame 18. |
| Turbo reserve | After each battle update the dashboard caps each human driver’s fuel at ten reserve charges and clears the partially used charge when the calculated gauge reaches 100. It displays 99 at capacity. A reference test compares the gauge and complete player record for 56 fuel combinations with the JP dashboard code. Arena combat and both player slots on the six duel stages exercise the cap through their normal update paths. |
| Combat HUD | The target sign and survivors list read all 13 vehicle labels from the JP executable, including its spacing controls. The target selector agrees with 96 JP calls covering ties, range limits, wrecks and inactive rivals. A pickup that fills the inventory, or a regular pickup attempted at capacity, displays the original capacity notice for 30 simulation updates. Player and AI inventory records and the notice countdown are compared with the retail pickup and HUD routines. |
| Combat music | Warehouse switches between CD tracks 4 and 7, River Park between 4 and 5, and Cyburbia between 6 and 7. AI firing requests, engagement and the original delays determine each switch. The reference test compares 290 updates per level with the JP executable, including Freeway as a fixed-track control. A River Park run on Metal confirmed both transitions and playback on the new track. |
| Moving pedestrians | The DMD registers one pedestrian in Warehouse, Freeway and Rooftops and three each in River Park and Cyburbia. Each draws a route and direction from the battle RNG during stage loading. Actors patrol and turn at the route endpoints. Their type determines how they react to a nearby car and whether they can fire or take damage. The JP comparison follows 2,000 updates per stage and checks coordinates, rotation, visibility, animation branches, random state, shots, the 55/56-unit hit boundary and respawn. Restart restores the stage-entry poses. A temporary tracking camera on Metal verified the skater on the River Park rink. |
| Hovering mercenaries | Warehouse and River Park use the machine gunner. Freeway and Cyburbia use the missile launcher. Rooftops runs both, with separate landing sites and roof heights. The collision scene registers the disc models and the renderer draws their selected sprite branches. Hits interrupt the cycle, trigger effects and delay the next descent. A 2,200-update test per stage compares position, animation branch, bullets, missiles and BIOS random state with the JP executable through landing, attack, retreat, a forced hit and recovery. A temporary tracking camera on Metal verified the Freeway launcher during descent. Both game modes issue the shared gun play/stop and fire-missile launch requests in mercenary slot order. The hover motor request comes last and uses the nearest source, keeping the first on a tie. A JP queue test covers 75 combinations of active slots, firing, launching and distance. The five stage replays also compare the flags passed to turnOnHoverCopSounds on each update. A campaign mixer test checks that stopping the mercenary gun leaves the player’s gun running. The Rooftops Metal capture with Outlaw is stored at `local/rooftops-hover-queue.png`. |
| Stationary police | Warehouse and Freeway contain three retail model types with machine-gun or missile fire. For each type, a 5,600-update comparison runs the JP executable at the detection boundary, moves the player into range and then away, and hits the officer. It compares model selection and random state after each update, bullet and missile positions during firing, and recovery after 900 scheduled updates. The Warehouse model was inspected on Metal with a temporary fixed camera and a scripted player position for the proximity check. |
| Destructible arena targets | All eleven single-player databases use the retail target order, instance numbers, positions and starting health. The registry includes 4 entries in Arena and Warehouse, 3 on Freeway, 12 in River Park, 25 in Cyburbia and 45 on Rooftops. A fixed-camera Metal capture verifies the roof before and after a scripted hit. Pedestrians and mercenaries use separate simulation systems. |
| Rooftop window | In ROOF1 and ROOF2, hitting the intact pane changes the collision branch and starts the nested glass animation. Further impacts leave it broken until a stage restart. | Arithmetic | 8,192 matrix, 8,192 normalization and 17,759 square-root cases compared with the original executable, including signed overflow in the sum of squares. |

Reproduce from the port root with the supported absolute CUE path exported as `TM1_CUE`.

```sh
cargo test --release --offline --locked --manifest-path crates/tm1-core/Cargo.toml -- --include-ignored --nocapture
cargo test --offline --locked --manifest-path crates/tm1-assets/Cargo.toml -- --include-ignored --nocapture
cargo test --offline --locked -- --include-ignored --nocapture
cargo run --offline --locked -- "$TM1_CUE" --demo-screenshot local/arena-combat.png
cargo run --offline --locked -- "$TM1_CUE" --realtime-screenshot local/realtime.png
cargo run --offline --locked -- "$TM1_CUE" --object-mesh --demo-screenshot local/object-mesh.png
```

Inputs, initial conditions, original entry addresses and comparisons are in the tests. [Core output](../evidence/drive-original.txt), [asset output](../evidence/asset-tests.txt) and [frontend output](../evidence/frontend-tests.txt) record the runs. Images under `local/` contain disc-derived material and stay out of version control.
