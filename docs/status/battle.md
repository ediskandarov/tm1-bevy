# Battle systems and effects

## Battle radar

Radar arrows and instrument sprites sample one source texel per logical screen pixel. Their dimensions include the final texel as in the JP sprite parser at 0x8013872c. A rectangle crossing a texture-page edge wraps inside that page.

The single-player radar collects opponents during their individual updates and active repair stands before regeneration. It displays the previous simulation pass, retaining up to twelve marks and four directional flags while hidden. Repair stands blink with the original radar buffer. A disc-backed test compares the buffered marks, colors, coordinates and edge flags with the JP executable across hide and show transitions, including a full buffer. The Cyburbia Metal capture at `local/cyburbia-radar.png` shows Road Kill at update 40. Framebuffer blending has not been compared pixel by pixel.

## Camera and HUD controls

Across 37,320 simulation updates on the six campaign levels, the bonus arena and the Minion encounter, the selected rival slot or absence of a target matched the JP closest-car routine after every update. A disc-backed test runs the JP dashboard from closest-target selection through insertion of the rival health polygon. It checks the two right-hand X coordinates, all four corner colors and whether an inactive rival submits a polygon, across 80 combinations of health, tier and activity. Both dashboards evaluate the health gradient at each logical screen column and reduce the interpolated command color to RGB555. The fixed-point color step and rounding follow the [DuckStation software rasterizer](https://github.com/stenzek/duckstation/blob/master/src/core/gpu_sw_rasterizer.inl). Final blending still uses the host framebuffer.

Disc-backed tests compare the speed display against the JP font request for all values from 0 through 2010 and three larger inputs. Above 999 the original fills all three places, including leading zeroes. Tests also compare the glyphs, positions and submission order of ammunition and turbo counters against the original sprite packets. The speed conversion preserves 32-bit multiplication overflow at the limits of the velocity field.

Single-player battle samples C, Z, V, L and B on simulation updates. The JP CheckVRMode routine gives priority to forward camera, reverse camera, radar, remaining cars and mirror, in that order. Holding any of the five controls keeps the group latched until an update sees all five released. A 128-update comparison executes the JP input branches for all 32 key combinations, including holds and overlapping changes. It checks camera mode, radar, remaining cars, mirror and latch state on every update. A separate keyboard integration test cycles the camera with the helicopter unlocked. Radar and remaining-car visibility carry into the next campaign stage. Cycling past either end of the view list with the selected special trigger held updates the original camera-combination flags and timer. A JP comparison checks all two-button pad combinations in four control layouts. It also follows the special-trigger timer for 33 car updates, including the exact controller word that clears the secondary flag.

## Repair stands and camera timing

In single-player battle the stand processes health after projectile impacts and before view input. A repair step that reaches the health cap exactly still holds the car. The next increment above the cap releases it and samples held camera and HUD controls on that same update. The ordinary camera holds its transition during repair. An active helicopter continues flying. In a duel, each car has its own repair result and camera transition. Warehouse tests compare the release update with the JP routine for both health limits and exercise each duel slot separately.

The four lightning faces on repair stands share the first face's texture origin. After missile movement and before hit lights, the simulation advances the JP effect clock once. A transition consumes one random draw for the next interval. Each active update then consumes four draws to select one of 21 texture frames per face. During the off interval, all corners use the origin. Both cameras read the resulting coordinates. Stage changes carry the effect clock, and a manual campaign restart restores its stage-entry phase.

Campaign encounter replays compare all 16 UV pairs against the face records modified by the JP executable on every update, alongside the existing car and RNG checks. A split-screen replay compares the same coordinates for 53 updates each on Warehouse, Arena and Rooftops, carrying the clock through the arena without a lightning object. The Minion replay runs the retail particle allocations because a full pool changes how many random draws a flamethrower burst consumes.

## Flamethrower launch audio

Each flamethrower sound consumes one entry in the 45-request battle queue. An accepted play replaces its previous tones. A discarded play leaves those tones alone. The campaign and duel mixers keep a playing flame tone in the requested group at its launch range until the sample finishes, the voice is displaced or its 960-update timer expires. A disc-backed test compares queue behavior with 41 through 46 preceding requests. A second test holds the emulated SDK key status on and checks the timer and release against 962 successive JP sound states.

A successful forward launch by Thumper or Minion plays the flamethrower and vehicle special from the collision model position plus the weapon direction. With all five emitters active, only the special plays, from the car position. A rear shot plays its launch sound even when the emitters are full and only consumes ammo if an emitter starts. A seven-shot JP replay checks both positions, request order and car state at several headings. The Rooftops boss replays check Minion’s sound positions during AI combat. Both game modes restart the shared flamethrower sound on each launch and replace each vehicle’s previous special voice. The disc program table supplies program 21 for the flamethrower, 49 for Thumper’s special and 56 for Minion’s special.

## Vehicle debris

Fragments use the two four-frame texture sequences selected by EXPLODE.c. The main camera sees fragments immediately after their physics update. The mirror and second duel view also see any debris created during the subsequent vehicle updates. Encounter replays compare position, animation selection and frame for both views. A separate JP rendering test checks the raw textured quad, its corners, UVs, texture page and palette across both sequences, including the original depth and screen cutoffs. The Arena replay with Yellow Jacket shows debris at the barrier on update 134 (`--demo-screenshot local/arena-car-fragments.png 134`).

## Ground fire

Wrecks and special-weapon impacts use the same eight-frame flame sequence. It repeats thirteen times over 104 updates. The renderer projects a strip between the impact position and a point 80 units above it using the original angle tables. A disc-backed test checks both cameras against the JP GPU commands over 105 updates, including the frame omitted from the secondary view in the final loop. The Warehouse capture uses Sweet Tooth with `--demo-special-screenshot local/warehouse-ground-fire.png 210`.

## Dropped weapons

Bombs, oil, spikes and catapults use the four weapon sprites in the arena DMD. Oil projects onto a 96-unit ground square. The other three face the camera. Each trap is drawn on its last active field, including the field when a car triggers it. Both battle modes play the launch and impact sounds from UACORE.VAB. Bombs produce a pair of bursts 50 world units apart vertically. Oil stays active after contact. Drop projection uses the current viewport lens, integer screen coordinates and the original center visibility bounds. A packet test runs the retail display routine for all four weapons, checks all four vertex positions, and exercises oil straddling the near plane with a rotated camera.

A disc-backed test launches each weapon, places the opponent at the trap, and compares the native car records, random state, impact request, and particle positions and call order with the JP executable. It checks which traps remain visible on the next update. A Metal capture of Road Kill deploying oil in Warehouse is saved at `local/warehouse-oil-near.png`. A second Metal capture at `local/warehouse-oil-packets.png` uses a temporary oil loadout to show the trap beneath Road Kill. Each quad uses its center depth divided by eight, with oil one bucket closer. A disc test reads the actual ordering-table link for each weapon on both sides of a bucket boundary.

Each camera assembles opaque geometry, blended geometry and combat sprites into one ordering table. The renderer sorts packets by bucket, game pass and reverse insertion order. Consecutive packets that use the same GPU blend operation share a draw call. A subtractive packet draws its STP texels and then its ordinary texels before the next packet. Main, mirror and duel views have independent queues. The JP depth-selection test checks triangle and quad clipping and fixed buckets. A second test walks mixed packets linked by JP AddPrim and checks vertex identity, shader mode and GPU blend operation in the submitted batches. A temporary three-panel Metal probe rendered additive red after subtractive red over black, reversed the order, and then replaced the result with an ordinary blue texel from a subtractive packet. RGB readback at the panel centers was (255, 0, 0), (0, 0, 0) and (0, 0, 255). The regular Metal build rendered Thumper’s flamethrower and both Warehouse duel views. Cyburbia ran 300 simulation steps with eight opponents in 15.0019 seconds.
