# Campaign, vehicles and stages

## Loss of life and Minion introduction

During the first 30 fields of the loss-of-life fade, holding Enter, Space or P skips to the next life and plays the menu confirmation sound. On the Minion introduction the same keys skip the five-second message. On a controller the accepted buttons are Cross, Select and Start. The retail boss screen initially waits for release on the upper controller halfword before polling confirmation on the lower halfword. An input integration test exercises both ports with virtual controllers, compares the decisions with the retail GetPadStatus and ctlpadAnySelect routines, and checks held Space during the loss-of-life fade.

## Reward and credits input

The campaign ending follows the two controller-port checks in screenPlayCarEnding and screenScrollTextInit. A held second controller button delays entry to the reward and credits screens. The reward waits for player one to release the controls before starting its five confirmation waits. Enter, Space and P map to confirmation. Holding confirmation through the last wait can skip the credits on their first input poll. A Bevy integration test exercises both release gates, holds Space across the transition and continues the hard campaign with the remaining life.

## Single-player pause input

In both modes, selecting AUDIO plays the confirmation sound immediately. The pause screen stays visible and the stage music stays paused until the participating players release all mapped buttons. This follows the release loop before screenAudioOptions in the JP executable. Keyboard integration tests cover a held Enter in the campaign and a second player holding a driving key in a duel.

The pause menu waits for the mapped controls to be released on entry, between choices, and before returning to battle. Overlapping inputs leave the selection in place until release. Controller options highlight the active arrow while the direction is held. Enter or Space confirms from the keyboard, and P retains its resume shortcut. A keyboard integration test holds overlapping inputs during navigation and checks the active arrow, layout, pause state and simulation frame through release.

## Title options

Up and Down select OPTIONS or 1P CONTEST on the title screen. AUDIO CONTROLS changes the paired music and effects levels and plays seven CD tracks in the retail menu order. The selected mix applies to the title music, garage, opponent deal and subsequent battle. CONTEST HISTORY reads HISTORY.TXT and leads into the vehicle showcase on natural completion. STAFF ROLL scrolls CREDITS.TXT over SCRNROAD.TIM. Both paths return to the options menu. A keyboard integration test checks track wraparound, history and staff-roll navigation, and carries the new mix into a battle. The Metal audio-screen capture used a temporary direct entry into the track selector.

## Single-player battle setup

Starting a contest or advancing a stage plays the original stage announcement and deals the selected opponents' indexed vehicle cards over the preceding scene. The deal consumes one BIOS random value per opponent before the battle initializes the cars. The animation advances at 60 fields per second. Its initial GetPadStatus(0) wait reads the second controller port. A victory interrupts CD music and replaces the battle voices with the centered player engine until the next arena opens. Each eleven-field card cue restarts sound ID 0x88 with the selected effects level, including the final cue after the last moving card. A Warehouse replay checks all four cues, their stereo samples, the input gate and the engine and CD handoff. The renderer rotates the four textured corners with the SDK fixed-point table and draws the two FT4 triangles using their original 0–127 and 0–63 texture coordinates. A JP screenDrawCard replay checks all four outgoing vertex positions at every moving and settled step for the 31 card placements across all seven stages. The card palette applies the draw command’s gain of 127/128 to each five-bit channel before RGBA expansion. Only a zero source palette word makes a texel transparent. The native Freeway capture at `local/freeway-card-colors.png` shows the five cards during the deal. Pixel-level rasterization has not been compared against a PlayStation capture. The host loads stage data synchronously, so disc seek times are not reproduced. To inspect a five-card deal, use `--garage --level freeway --demo-transition-screenshot local/freeway-opponents.png 45`.

Selecting a vehicle opens SCRNE21P.TIM with its 128 × 64 indexed card, followed by START, OPTIONS and EXIT. OPTIONS uses the disc difficulty labels and four control diagrams. Changing the layout before the encounter configures the core and keyboard mapping. The password editor and retry screen use the retail text positions, colors and blinking underscore. P and D in the garage remain keyboard shortcuts. Disc-backed tests follow the normal menu path into Arena 5 with a changed difficulty and control layout, and check both setup idle deadlines. To capture the battle card, run with `--garage --car outlaw --demo-battle-screenshot local/battle-outlaw.png 50`.

## Warehouse

Launch with `--level warehouse` to drive WH1 or WH2, selected by the original vehicle-dependent shell rules, against all three original opponents for the selected player. The game reads the level geometry, sky and textures from the disc, spawns the player at the original Warehouse start, and opens with CDDA track 4. Warehouse uses its own trigger points, bridges, curbs, potholes and AI lane selection. The encounter can be restarted from the keyboard. The HUD shows the nearest surviving opponent and the number of rivals left. The round ends after all three rivals are defeated or the player is wrecked. Winning advances to Freeway.

## Rooftops

After the third rival falls, the simulation continues through the original 180-field delay and spawns Minion on the roof selected by the player’s ground group. A player below height 6,400 respawns on the starting roof. The fourth opponent uses front and rear machine guns and a special that combines two swarm missiles, a projectile, a taser and a front flamethrower. R restarts the three-rival opening. A Metal capture with all three rivals set to zero health at startup showed Minion at 250 health and one survivor out of four. ## Single-player campaign

A contest starts with three lives. The first five victories advance through Warehouse, Freeway, City Park, Cyburbia and Rooftops in the same window. Each stage loads its own collision geometry, textures, opponent lineup, audio voices and CD track. A victory waits for the original 180-field return delay to expire. The new stage restores health, resets gun heat, grants 20 special charges and retains ordinary ammunition and the selected weapon. A manual restart replays that stage entry. The port uses reproducible RNG across stages. The retail shell also samples a hardware timer.

On player death, simulation continues through the return delay. While lives remain, the player respawns in the same encounter with the default weapon loadout. Respawn restores the default light environment on each car, including frozen rivals. Active hit timers continue, so a later update can flash a car again. A nine-model test compares that sequence with the JP level initialization and light update routines. On Freeway, a separate comparison displaces all four barricades and verifies that their collision and rendering switches retain the displacement through player respawn. The terminal defeat freezes after the third loss. Visual checks on Metal used a fixture that started the opening opponents at zero health to trigger the Arena-to-Warehouse transition and the Rooftops introduction.
